# Combat interaction

The portrait opens Character, the zodiac opens the ability map, and ten quick slots select commands. Only the right panel confirms a command. Selection and free assignments do not advance the timeline.

`GameState.Loadout` owns the current player's command definitions, carried items, committed equipment, focus, and shortcuts. Wait yields until the next living character's scheduled Turn + 0.01, ignoring effect events; intrinsic LeftHandStrike and RightHandStrike commands costing 1.0 Turn and dealing 1 damage. No progression rules have been added. Item actions and additional learned/unlearned nodes render from the loadout's definitions when authored.

A command identifies its operation and optional required item and slot. Definitions supply execution effects, target rules, turn/focus cost, and icon. The default targeted rule preserves the former front-enemy restriction; an authored target rule can override it. Missing equipment and resources preserve assignments while disabling execution. Confirmation rechecks current target state and requirements in gameplay.

Character edits use an independent equipment dictionary. Each slot whose final item differs costs 0.6 Turn (including removal and both slots of a move); reverting all changes costs zero. The compact Character dialog shows Close for an unchanged loadout, or Confirm/Cancel and the total Turn cost while changes are pending. Confirmation validates ownership/slots and current actor, applies equipment changes in one timeline action, and closes only on success. Cancel, Escape, and Back discard the working dictionary. Inventory ordering and shortcuts persist independently. The dialog keeps its input scope until the next update after dismissal to prevent the closing event reaching the scene.

Validation: `dotnet run --project tests/CombatTimelineChecks --no-restore` and `dotnet run --project tests/TargetSelectionChecks --no-restore`. These cover resource failures, invalid targets, retained shortcuts, reentrant confirmation, transaction failure, equipment commit, zero-cost reversion, and target-independent commands. Visual layout still requires an in-game check.

Status definitions live in `Database/StatusEffects.cs` and are exposed through `Storyline.StatusEffects`. `GameState.StatusEffects` and `CharacterData.StatusEffects` hold typed IDs. Each definition supplies its name, brief description, and HUD icon; the Character dialog renders each icon to the left of its name and description while compact HUDs retain icons. Guarded, Inspired, and Focused preserve the previous three display placeholders; their descriptions are provisional and do not implement combat modifiers. An empty player list displays �No status effects�.

The Zodiac dialog currently presents a fixed three-column, five-row grid with four placeholder abilities: Slash, Push, Parry, and Blade Storm. Each entry displays an icon, name, and brief description from `Storyline.Abilities`, authored in `Database/Abilities.cs`. Remaining cells are empty. The shorter dialog is aligned near the top to expose the center HUD action slots. Drag an ability onto a slot to assign it for free; a drop outside the slots leaves assignments unchanged. Close remains its only button. These presentation placeholders do not grant combat commands or implement their effects.

Drag input checks: `dotnet run --project tests/AbilityDragChecks`. Placeholder ability assignments remain unavailable for execution until their combat cost and behavior are authored.

The center HUD holds ten 56-pixel square action frames, separated by 4 pixels and aligned to the right beside the Zodiac opener. Slots display ability icons, dim unavailable commands, and highlight the selected command. Drag targets follow the actual frame bounds.

An action occupies at most one slot. Assigning it elsewhere clears its previous slot and replaces the destination assignment; invalid assignments leave all slots unchanged.

Character items support the same cursor-centered drag gesture as abilities. Drop on a compatible equipment slot to edit the pending loadout. An equipped item exchanges with the target item if both fit their resulting slots; an item dragged from inventory replaces the target while the displaced item remains owned in inventory. Invalid drops leave the layout unchanged. Confirm applies the final slot changes and Turn cost; Cancel discards them.

LeftHandStrike and RightHandStrike are intrinsic commands, separate from the ability DB and learned abilities. Drag either hand equipment slot onto the action bar to assign its strike, including an empty hand. Empty-hand drops outside the action bar do nothing. Each strike currently costs 1.0 Turn and deals one General base damage point plus weapon bonuses to a valid front-row enemy, with no Focus cost. Confirmation resolves the currently committed item in the chosen hand, or an empty fist; equipment changes never invalidate these assignments. Item-specific attack rules remain to be authored.

Items and carried items can have optional `WeaponData.Damage`, mapping each `DamageType` to its complete point count, including General. `Sharp` adds one Slashing point if the weapon already has Slashing damage. A resolved `HandStrike.DamagePoints` snapshots the complete weapon damage composition, or one General point for fists and non-weapons; `Damage` is their total, applied once. Fists and non-weapons deal only the General point. Ordinary weapons deal 3 total damage: Scimitar: 1 General + 2 Slashing; Sharp scimitar: 1 General + 3 Slashing; spiked mace: 1 General + 1 Crushing + 1 Piercing. `DamageProfile.Negate` removes all matching points, while `Reduce` removes a specified number. General points can also be negated or reduced by selecting DamageType.General. Defense/status rules are not wired to these operations yet.

`DamageCategory` groups specific types into Physical, Elemental, Spiritual, Mental, and Magical. `DamageType.GetCategory()` maps Slashing, Piercing, and Crushing to Physical; other categories are available for future specific types. `DamageProfile.AmountIn(category)` counts matching typed points, excluding General, which has no category.

The player starts with a Sword equipped in the right hand (+2 Slashing), an empty left hand, RightHandStrike in action slot 1, and LeftHandStrike in action slot 2. Throw starts in action slot 3 and Wait in slot 10; slots 4–9 start empty. Initial equipment is committed during loadout creation without a Turn charge. Wait can be freely reassigned.

Weapons have a `WeaponRange` (Short by default). Short Strikes target only the front-most occupied enemy row. Medium Strikes target the fixed Front/Middle rows, falling back to Back only when both are empty. Long Strikes can target any living enemy position. Empty fists and non-weapons use Short. Live enemy occupancy is recalculated for target indicators and confirmation, excluding defeated/removed enemies and allies.

`ItemData` and `CarriedItem` expose an `Icons` array and zero-based `IconIndex`. Their computed `Icon` selects the variant used by inventory, equipment, drag previews, and hand strikes. Indices wrap using modulo, including negative indices; an empty list keeps the existing placeholder behavior. Variants do not change weapon stats or item identity.

Combat outcomes are Victory (all configured victory conditions), Defeat (any lose condition), or Escape. Defeat takes priority when conditions coincide. The default scenario victory condition is elimination of all living enemies; the default authored defeat condition is player defeat/removal. Escape spends 1.0 Turn and resolves intervening turns/events normally, then flees on the player�s next occurrence if no earlier outcome has ended combat. Each scenario can route outcomes to separate story nodes. The opening encounter routes to three placeholder outcome nodes/screens, and `GameState.CurrentStoryNodeId` and `LastCombatOutcome` record the transition. The Escape button and icon use the shared tan color.

Scenarios author `VictoryConditions` and `DefeatConditions` arrays explicitly. Both are evaluated after each character action, before scheduling another turn; all victory predicates or any defeat predicate choose the corresponding story route. Default reusable predicates are `CombatConditions.AllEnemiesDefeated` and `CombatConditions.PlayerDefeated`, using live health as well as combatant membership. Escape routes independently on the fleeing player�s next turn, after checking defeat/victory.

A character placement can set `RequiredForVictory`. Each marked character adds a defeat predicate to the scenario�s victory conditions; unmarked characters do not. The first encounter marks only the enemy in BackLeft. Defeating that enemy wins while the front/middle enemies may remain. If a scenario defines neither explicit predicates nor marked characters, it falls back to defeating all enemies.


WeaponCatalog defines Sword (+2 Slashing, seven icons), Axe (+1 Slashing and +1 Crushing, five icons), Knives (+1 Piercing and +1 Slashing, five knife icons), Clubs (+2 Crushing, two club icons and one hammer icon), Maces (+1 Crushing and +1 Piercing, two icons), and Flail (+1 Crushing and +1 Piercing, two icons). Each adds these bonuses to the one General base point for 3 total damage. WeaponCatalog.Create selects a type and icon variant when creating a carried weapon. The starting Sword uses this catalog. These melee types use Short range. Pistol uses Long range and deals exactly 3 Piercing with no General damage; it currently uses the placeholder icon.

Throw is an intrinsic long-range basic action assigned to slot 3 in new loadouts. Confirm opens a focused, paginated item picker without spending Turn. Choose an item to commit immediately; Cancel/Escape/Back retain the action and target without changing combat state. The Character dialog can assign Throw to another quick slot.

`ItemData.ThrowDamage` and `CarriedItem.ThrowDamage` contain independent discrete damage profiles; absence means ineligible. Initial catalog content gives Sword 1 Piercing and Knives 2 Piercing when thrown, without Strike/Sharp bonuses. Other catalog weapons have no Throw profile. Throw shares the Strike action cost and damage application path, with no hit roll or variation. Basic action icons use the `actions` asset group, including the moved Fist texture; Throw uses the dedicated Throw texture.

Eligible equipped melee weapons can be thrown with occupied hands. Unequipped inventory items require a free hand. A `WeaponData.TwoHanded` weapon occupies both hand slots and frees both when thrown. Inventory stacks use `CarriedItem.Quantity`; a Throw removes one unit. Single-use `ThrowSelection` sessions revalidate actor, target, ownership, location, profile, and hand access before submitting one combat action. Removed units remain in `CombatLoadout.ThrownItems` for future disposition/recovery rules. Normal Character equipment changes retain per-slot costs.

Throw checks in CombatTimelineChecks cover long-range targeting, independent damage, read-only opening/cancellation, duplicate selection, stacked items, two-handed removal, blocked inventory access, and stale target/ownership/hand state. In-game picker layout still needs visual validation.

Wait is an intrinsic, target-free command using `actions.wait.png`. Its actual timeline cost is calculated at confirmation from the next pending living character (ally or enemy), excluding the acting player and non-character effects. The player resumes at that character's scheduled Turn + 0.01; effects in between resolve normally. With no next character, Wait cannot be confirmed and spends nothing. The Character dialog supports assigning Wait to a quick slot. Opponents' placeholder 0.6-Turn pass behavior remains separate from the player's Wait action.

Wait does not force the player to be the next actor: all characters scheduled before the new player Turn act through the normal timeline comparator. Characters tied at the next Turn, and characters between that Turn and Turn + 0.01, can therefore all act before the player resumes. Existing initiative/team/row/random/sequence tie breakers remain in effect.

Character now displays Throw and Wait as full-color icons in 56-pixel item frames at the bottom-left of the footer, opposite Close/Cancel/Confirm. Drag an icon onto an action-bar slot to assign it for free; releasing elsewhere leaves shortcuts and equipment unchanged. The footer no longer expands into assignment buttons for these basic actions. The equipment Turn cost is centered below the equipment slots. Close and Confirm have matching 24-pixel right and bottom margins; Cancel sits beside Confirm.
