# Combat interaction

The portrait opens Character, the zodiac opens the ability map, and ten quick slots select commands. Only the right panel confirms a command. Selection and free assignments do not advance the timeline.

`GameState.Loadout` owns the current player's command definitions, carried items, committed equipment, focus, and shortcuts. Wait yields until the next living character's scheduled Turn + 0.01, ignoring effect events; intrinsic LeftHandStrike and RightHandStrike commands costing 1.0 Turn and dealing 1 damage. No progression rules have been added. Item actions and additional learned/unlearned nodes render from the loadout's definitions when authored.

A command identifies its operation and optional required item and slot. Definitions supply execution effects, target rules, turn/focus cost, and icon. The default targeted rule preserves the former front-enemy restriction; an authored target rule can override it. Missing equipment and resources preserve assignments while disabling execution. Confirmation rechecks current target state and requirements in gameplay.

Character edits use an independent complete item layout: 20 fixed inventory cells plus 9 equipment slots. Items owns the item records; Inventory exposes exactly 20 nullable item/stack cells, excluding equipped items. Each slot whose final item differs costs 0.6 Turn, including inventory cells, removal, and both ends of a move; reverting all changes costs zero. The compact Character dialog shows Close for an unchanged loadout, or Confirm/Cancel and the total Turn cost while changes are pending. Confirmation validates ownership/slots and current actor, applies equipment changes in one timeline action, and closes only on success. Cancel, Escape, and Back discard the working dictionary. Inventory positions are committed with equipment; shortcuts persist independently. The dialog keeps its input scope until the next update after dismissal to prevent the closing event reaching the scene.

Validation: `dotnet run --project tests/CombatTimelineChecks --no-restore` and `dotnet run --project tests/TargetSelectionChecks --no-restore`. These cover resource failures, invalid targets, retained shortcuts, reentrant confirmation, transaction failure, equipment commit, zero-cost reversion, and target-independent commands. Visual layout still requires an in-game check.

Status definitions live in `Database/StatusEffects.cs` and are exposed through `Storyline.StatusEffects`. `GameState.StatusEffects` and `CharacterData.StatusEffects` hold typed IDs. Each definition supplies its name, brief description, and HUD icon; the Character dialog renders each icon to the left of its name and description while compact HUDs retain icons. Guarded, Inspired, and Focused preserve the previous three display placeholders; their descriptions are provisional and do not implement combat modifiers. An empty player list displays �No status effects�.

The Zodiac dialog currently presents a fixed three-column, five-row grid with four placeholder abilities: Slash, Push, Parry, and Blade Storm. Each entry displays an icon, name, and brief description from `Storyline.Abilities`, authored in `Database/Abilities.cs`. Remaining cells are empty. The shorter dialog is aligned near the top to expose the center HUD action slots. Drag an ability onto a slot to assign it for free; a drop outside the slots leaves assignments unchanged. Close remains its only button. These presentation placeholders do not grant combat commands or implement their effects.

Drag input checks: `dotnet run --project tests/AbilityDragChecks`. Placeholder ability assignments remain unavailable for execution until their combat cost and behavior are authored.

The center HUD holds ten 56-pixel square action frames, separated by 4 pixels and aligned to the right beside the Zodiac opener. Slots display ability icons, dim unavailable commands, and highlight the selected command. Drag targets follow the actual frame bounds.

An action occupies at most one slot. Assigning it elsewhere clears its previous slot and replaces the destination assignment; invalid assignments leave all slots unchanged.

Character items support the same cursor-centered drag gesture as abilities. Every inventory and equipment cell is a drop target, including empty inventory cells. Drag a whole item/stack to an empty cell to relocate it, or to an occupied cell to swap if both items fit. Weapons and other hand-held items can use either hand. Two-handed weapons occupy both hand cells; displaced occupants return to the source cell and, when necessary, free inventory cells. Capacity failures and incompatible swaps leave the layout unchanged. Confirm applies all locations in one action; Cancel/Escape/Back discard the preview. Inventory cells retain holes and never compact when items are moved or consumed. TryAddItem enforces the 20-cell capacity for new carried records.

LeftHandStrike and RightHandStrike are intrinsic commands, separate from the ability DB and learned abilities. Drag either hand equipment slot onto the action bar to assign its strike, including an empty hand. Empty-hand drops outside the action bar do nothing. Each strike currently costs 1.0 Turn and deals one General base damage point plus weapon bonuses to a valid front-row enemy, with no Focus cost. Confirmation resolves the currently committed item in the chosen hand, or an empty fist; equipment changes never invalidate these assignments. Item-specific attack rules remain to be authored.

Items and carried items can have optional `WeaponData.Damage`, mapping each `DamageType` to its complete point count, including General. `Sharp` adds one Slashing point if the weapon already has Slashing damage. A resolved `HandStrike.DamagePoints` snapshots the complete weapon damage composition, or one General point for fists and non-weapons; `Damage` is their total, applied once. Fists and non-weapons deal only the General point. Ordinary weapons deal 3 total damage: Scimitar: 1 General + 2 Slashing; Sharp scimitar: 1 General + 3 Slashing; spiked mace: 1 General + 1 Crushing + 1 Piercing. `DamageProfile.Negate` removes all matching points, while `Reduce` removes a specified number. General points can also be negated or reduced by selecting DamageType.General. Defense/status rules are not wired to these operations yet.

`DamageCategory` groups specific types into Physical, Elemental, Spiritual, Mental, and Magical. `DamageType.GetCategory()` maps Slashing, Piercing, and Crushing to Physical; Poison also maps to Physical, while Fire, Ice, and Lightning map to Elemental. `DamageProfile.AmountIn(category)` counts matching typed points, excluding General, which has no category.

The player starts with a Sword equipped in the right hand (+2 Slashing), an empty left hand, RightHandStrike in action slot 1, and LeftHandStrike in action slot 2. Wait starts in slot 10; slots 3–9 start empty. Initial equipment is committed during loadout creation without a Turn charge. The hand attacks and Wait are pinned.

Weapons have a `WeaponRange` (Short by default). Short Strikes target only the front-most occupied enemy row. Medium Strikes target the fixed Front/Middle rows, falling back to Back only when both are empty. Long Strikes can target any living enemy position. Empty fists and non-weapons use Short. Live enemy occupancy is recalculated for target indicators and confirmation, excluding defeated/removed enemies and allies.

`ItemData` and `CarriedItem` expose an `Icons` array and zero-based `IconIndex`. Their computed `Icon` selects the variant used by inventory, equipment, drag previews, and hand strikes. Indices wrap using modulo, including negative indices; an empty list keeps the existing placeholder behavior. Variants do not change weapon stats or item identity.

Combat outcomes are Victory (all configured victory conditions), Defeat (any lose condition), or Escape. Defeat takes priority when conditions coincide. The default scenario victory condition is elimination of all living enemies; the default authored defeat condition is player defeat/removal. Escape spends 1.0 Turn and resolves intervening turns/events normally, then flees on the player�s next occurrence if no earlier outcome has ended combat. Each scenario can route outcomes to separate story nodes. The opening encounter routes to three placeholder outcome nodes/screens, and `GameState.CurrentStoryNodeId` and `LastCombatOutcome` record the transition. The Escape button and icon use the shared tan color.

Scenarios author `VictoryConditions` and `DefeatConditions` arrays explicitly. Both are evaluated after each character action, before scheduling another turn; all victory predicates or any defeat predicate choose the corresponding story route. Default reusable predicates are `Rules.VictoryRules.AllEnemiesDefeated` and `Rules.DefeatRules.PlayerDefeated`, using live health as well as combatant membership. Escape routes independently on the fleeing player�s next turn, after checking defeat/victory.

A character placement can set `RequiredForVictory`. Each marked character adds a defeat predicate to the scenario�s victory conditions; unmarked characters do not. The first encounter marks only the enemy in BackLeft. Defeating that enemy wins while the front/middle enemies may remain. If a scenario defines neither explicit predicates nor marked characters, it falls back to defeating all enemies.


WeaponCatalog defines Sword (+2 Slashing, seven icons), Axe (+1 Slashing and +1 Crushing, five icons), Knives (+1 Piercing and +1 Slashing, five knife icons), Clubs (+2 Crushing, two club icons and one hammer icon), Maces (+1 Crushing and +1 Piercing, two icons), and Flail (+1 Crushing and +1 Piercing, two icons). Each adds these bonuses to the one General base point for 3 total damage. WeaponCatalog.Create selects a type and icon variant when creating a carried weapon. The starting Sword uses this catalog. These melee types use Short range. Pistol, Bow, Crossbow, and Sling use Long range and their dedicated icon variants (3 pistols, 3 bows, 1 crossbow, 1 sling). They deal only Piercing damage: 3 for Pistol/Bow/Crossbow and 2 for Sling.

Throw is an intrinsic long-range action prepared with a specific item in the Character dialog. Preparation costs no Turn. Closing selects the action; selecting a valid target and confirming spends the Throw cost and removes one unit of the prepared item. Failed confirmation preserves the item and its prepared selection. Throw is not a quick-slot action.

`ItemData.ThrowDamage` and `CarriedItem.ThrowDamage` contain independent discrete damage profiles; absence means ineligible. Initial catalog content gives Sword 1 Piercing and Knives 2 Piercing when thrown, without Strike/Sharp bonuses. Other catalog weapons have no Throw profile. Throw shares the Strike action cost and damage application path, with no hit roll or variation. Basic action icons use the `actions` asset group, including the moved Fist texture; Throw uses the dedicated Throw texture.

Eligible equipped melee weapons can be thrown with occupied hands. Unequipped inventory items require a free hand. A `WeaponData.TwoHanded` weapon occupies both hand slots and frees both when thrown. Inventory stacks use `CarriedItem.Quantity`; a Throw removes one unit. Single-use `ThrowSelection` sessions revalidate actor, target, ownership, location, profile, and hand access before submitting one combat action. Removed units remain in `CombatLoadout.ThrownItems` for future disposition/recovery rules. Normal Character equipment changes retain per-slot costs.

Throw checks in CombatTimelineChecks cover long-range targeting, independent damage, read-only opening/cancellation, duplicate selection, stacked items, two-handed removal, blocked inventory access, and stale target/ownership/hand state. The Character drop frame still needs in-game visual validation.

Wait is an intrinsic, target-free command using `actions.wait.png`. Its actual timeline cost is calculated at confirmation from the next pending living character (ally or enemy), excluding the acting player and non-character effects. The player resumes at that character's scheduled Turn + 0.01; effects in between resolve normally. With no next character, Wait cannot be confirmed and spends nothing. Wait is pinned in slot 10 and is not shown in the Character dialog. Opponents' placeholder 0.6-Turn pass behavior remains separate from the player's Wait action.

Wait does not force the player to be the next actor: all characters scheduled before the new player Turn act through the normal timeline comparator. Characters tied at the next Turn, and characters between that Turn and Turn + 0.01, can therefore all act before the player resumes. Existing initiative/team/row/random/sequence tie breakers remain in effect.

Character displays one Throw item frame in the footer. Drop a throwable item into it to prepare Throw for activation on close; click the frame to clear the pending selection. Throw cannot be assigned to quick slots. Choose a target and confirm in the HUD to spend Turn and throw that specific item, with no second item picker. Wait and Reload have no frames. The Turn cost label uses the same bottom margin and vertical text center as Close/Cancel/Confirm.

The player's starting quick slots 1 and 2 (right/left hand Attack) and 10 (Wait) are pinned. Assignment validation rejects replacing these slots or moving their actions elsewhere; slots 3–9 remain customizable. Assigning a pinned action to its existing slot is harmless. Assignment-choice buttons disable incompatible pinned destinations, and drag/drop uses the same validation.

Ranged weapons start with one loaded shot. Shooting uses the hand attack command, consumes that shot, and costs the weapon's AttackSpeed: Pistol 0.1, Bow 0.4, Crossbow 0.2, Sling 0.6 Turn. Empty weapons cannot shoot. Drop matching ammunition from inventory onto a weapon in the Character equipment slots to queue a reload. Pistol uses Bullet, Bow Arrow, Crossbow Bolt, and Sling Stone; WeaponCatalog.CreateAmmunition creates stacks with a placeholder pouch icon. Reload costs Pistol 1.4, Bow 0.8, Crossbow 2.1, Sling 0.8 Turn and restores the shot without firing. Reload imposes no hand-availability requirement. Pending reloads add to the displayed equipment Turn cost, commit together in one action on Confirm, and consume ammunition only on success; Cancel/Escape/Back discard them. Invalid ammo, insufficient stacks, and stale equipment leave the transaction unchanged. Bow and Crossbow occupy both hands and require both to shoot; Pistol and Sling can shoot one-handed. Loaded shots belong to each item within its combat loadout and persist across unequipping and reequipping. Melee attacks keep their existing 1 Turn cost.

Movement checks cover inventory-to-hand, hand-to-hand, hand-to-inventory, inventory swaps and whole-stack relocation, two-handed displacement with a full bag, incompatible or duplicate placements, cancellation, reverted costs, and reload consumption without shifting neighboring cells. Empty item frames use the same press-origin click recognition as drag sources so releasing over a target cannot rebuild the dialog before the source drop executes.

Character keeps item manipulation in drag/drop and has no selected-item action buttons or expanding inspection footer. Drag activation requires 6 pixels of movement. Character and Zodiac previews use Prelude.DragItem, a GameObject2D with SpriteRenderer and one centered SpriteInstance in DefaultUI at draw order 30000. It follows the captured mouse’s latest position each update and on movement, bypassing GUI elements and layout margins. Drop, dialog close, and combat outcome remove the preview; a disconnected mouse hides it.

Dropping an item into Throw immediately selects the pending Throw action and shows that item’s icon in the HUD confirmation panel. Closing commits the prepared selection; clearing the frame or cancelling restores the prior action. Prepared Throw commands continue to show the selected item’s icon after closing.

Character inventory, equipment, and Throw slots retain their controls while items move. Drops update slot textures, quantities, pending reload text, cost, and confirmation controls in place rather than disposing and rebuilding the modal dialog. ItemSlot owns a TextureRenderer for its icon; changing the icon preserves aspect ratio without invalidating GUI layout. Footer text and visibility changes may still request layout. Drag handlers resolve the refreshed contents of their original slot on subsequent drags.

Damage sources call `DamageResolver.Apply(target.DamageReceiver, profile)`. The shared resolver applies optional mitigation, updates health, and emits `DamageTaken` with the resolved profile, health before/after, health lost, and lethal status. Zero damage and already-defeated targets emit no damage event. Overkill preserves the full resolved profile while health loss is capped at remaining health. Direct health changes remain available for initialization/healing and do not trigger damage feedback.

Character visuals subscribe to their receiver's damage events and queue the typed color tints independently of the source action. The combat scene separately observes lethal events to remove combatants from the timeline. Defeated artwork is hidden after feedback completes, and outcome scene transitions wait for pending damage feedback. New spells, ongoing damage, and hazards should use the same resolver without invoking presentation methods.

Effect overlays are separate sprite artwork displayed on a character. The term effect overlay covers visual effects, including future healing, buffs, and debuffs; these additional effect sources are not implemented yet. Character color tints are separate damage feedback. Currently, attack effects travel as optional `AttackImpact` metadata through the shared resolver. `ImpactOccurred` is emitted for living targets even when damage is fully mitigated, independently of `DamageTaken`. The held item carries `WeaponData.ImpactTexture`, a content ID consumed by the confirmed basic attack. Catalog defaults select `slash_01/02/03` for Sword/Axe/Knives, `impact_02/03/04` for Clubs/Maces/Flail, `ranged_01` for Pistol, `arrow_01/02` for Bow/Crossbow, and `ranged_02` for Sling (all in the `effects` group with `.png` source IDs). Unarmed strikes use `effects.impact_01.png`. Custom weapon data can override the effect for both melee and ranged attacks. Throws use the selected item's icon. Ranged strikes layer the loaded ammunition icon above the configured weapon effect; reload retains an immutable ammunition snapshot even if its inventory stack is consumed. Initially loaded ammunition uses the catalog's default icon (currently the pouch placeholder).

Each overlay appears over the target's torso for 500 ms, with rapid impacts queued. Effect sprite artwork fits within half the character width. Enemy characters do not play stagger animations. For allies, stagger begins during the final 17 ms of a damaging overlay, then continues after it disappears; blocked hits do not stagger. Damage without an overlay starts ally stagger immediately. Timing uses elapsed time, including updates that cross multiple feedback phases. Artwork uses separate untinted renderers; typed damage tints continue on the character sprite. Hiding defeated targets and outcome transitions wait for both kinds of feedback. Effects textures are built from `Prelude/.assets/Textures/Effects` under the `effects` content group.

When an attack inflicts no health loss, the resolver replaces its normal attack overlay (including item/ammunition icons) with `effects.blocked.png`. Fully mitigated hits produce blocked feedback without damage tints. Already-defeated targets still produce no feedback.

Combat feedback now uses Nexus.Core's generic `AnimationPlayer<T>`. Damage points hold their tint for 80 ms each and effect overlays last 500 ms. A lethal hit finishes both forms of hit feedback before playing a separate 250 ms defeat fade for the character and shadow. On completion every character renderer is hidden. The character remains attached to its formation with its definition and combat resources intact; scene transitions wait for the defeat playback as well. Hidden artwork is excluded from pixel hit testing.
